# Side B

Side B is a web experience where you walk into an illustrated record shop, step into a pressing booth at the back, build a custom vinyl record, and send it to someone as a link. The person who receives it opens a wrapped record-shop bag and plays the record on a fully working turntable.

This file is a handoff from the conversation where the project was designed. Read it before making changes.

## How the project got here

It started as a record store simulator, then went through a few ideas: a shareable "shelf of albums" gift, and finally a custom vinyl builder. The builder won because it gives people a clear reason to use it (a modern mixtape you make for someone) and it doesn't need a big catalogue or a database. The record shop is now the setting and the wrapping, not the product.

Ideas that were tried and dropped, and why:
- Hiding a YouTube player to play full songs: YouTube's rules require the player to stay visible, and ads can't be turned off. Use 30-second previews plus "listen to the full song" links instead.
- Deezer for search: not available in India. Apple's iTunes catalogue is used instead.
- Photorealism: hand-coded scenes can't reach it. A flat 2D editorial style with ink outlines felt fake and boring, and a soft night-time "lifelike" pass still looked 2D. The chosen look is a rich, sunlit illustration (see Style below), based on an illustration of the Ultima Thule record shop the owner supplied.

## Files

- `index.html` is the entire site: HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file.
- `api/search.js` is a Vercel function. The site calls `/api/search?q=...` and it asks Apple's iTunes Search API (country `IN`, songs only) and returns track name, artist, album, artwork, preview URL and an Apple Music link. Apple blocks direct browser calls, which is why this function exists.
- `api/albums.js` holds the list of real albums on the shop's shelves (Ariana Grande, Tame Impala, The Beatles, Mac Miller, Sade and many more) and can look up their cover art on iTunes. The covers themselves are **saved in the site** (`img/covers/`, 200 px) and listed in `index.html` between `/*SHELF*/` and `/*SHELF_END*/`, so the shelves show real covers from the first moment on every device. Waiting on Apple each visit was slow on phones and showed practice covers in the meantime. To change what's on the shelves, edit the list at the top of `api/albums.js`, then run `node scripts/fetch-covers.js` (it needs to reach `itunes.apple.com` and `is1-ssl` to `is5-ssl.mzstatic.com`; the `side-b` cloud environment allows them) and commit `img/covers/` and `index.html`. If the saved list is ever empty, the page falls back to asking the function at `/api/albums`.
- `api/record.js` is a Vercel function for short links. Pressing a record saves it in Upstash Redis for 30 days and gives a link like `/r/k3x9q2Ab`. The function also serves that page with a link preview (who it's from, the record's name, a picture of the sleeve). It allows 20 records per person per hour.
- `vercel.json` sends `/r/...` addresses to that function and bundles `index.html` with it.
- `package.json` only declares Node 18 or newer. There's no build step.
- `README.md` has deployment steps.

## The experience, in order

1. **Intro screen**: the name, one line ("Make a record for someone.") and one button, "Walk up to the shop". Keep it this short. The example gift is still reachable from code as `window.__example()`.
2. **Outside**: a New York record store at dusk in autumn, drawn in code (`facadeSVG`) and seen **straight on**, so nothing is skewed. The owner's references: a 1970s New York "Music Store" under a fire escape, and a brick record shop with a big white sign, records fixed to the wall and a bench. They like a high level of detail and want it to read as a vintage record store: glass door and windows, neon signs, some brutalist New York. Rejected along the way (don't bring any back): an AI-made picture; a flat cartoon front with crates on the ground; a modern glass "corporate" front with a lightbox sign, red seating and 2D shrubs; a daytime street with made-up gig posters, a café and a mural ("fake", "AI slop"); a corner shop seen at 45 degrees ("angled and short", skewed pavement, "soulless, brown and black", the same words repeated on every awning); and a quaint teal Victorian front with curtains, striped awnings, flower pots, pumpkins, patterned tiles and a thin tree ("not giving record store", too many patterns, the tree "fake and naked"). No invented posters, flyers or other shops, and say each thing once. What's drawn:
   - **The shop front** (x 250 to 1306): red painted cast-iron columns; a big cream sign with "SIDE B RECORDS" in bold red and one small line under it, a gold record and a black record fixed over its ends, three black barn lamps above. Two tall plain glass windows in black steel show the **real shop inside** (`<use href="#roomArt">`), with real album covers clipped along a wire and stood on the sill (`shopWin`, `cover`, slots from 200). **Neon** (`neonG`, filter `oNeon`): a yellow record and pink "Vinyl" script on the left, blue "PRESSING BOOTH" with a yellow arrow on the right, red "OPEN" in a blue outline over the door. The door is steel and glass with a push bar, between two side lights.
   - **Upstairs**: a tan brick tenement (`brickPat`), brownstone lintels and sills, plain windows with blinds (`sash`, `roomIn`: a lamp, a shelf or a TV's blue light; some dark; a few air conditioners), an iron **fire escape** with its shadow (`fesc`), a bracketed cornice and a wooden **water tower** on the roof, a moon and stars above (phones see all of this).
   - **The street**: on the left a raw concrete block (`brut`: board-marked concrete, fins, ribbon windows, a glazed lobby); on the right a red brick tenement with a roll-down shutter and its own fire escape (`ten`); the two alternate far enough for very wide screens. A concrete sidewalk in big slabs with a granite kerb and the road (`EP`, eye at (778, 560), ground at 880), warm, pink and blue light on it, a slatted bench under the left window, a sandwich board of real covers, a red hydrant, a wire litter bin and a tall street lamp.
   - **Autumn**: a street tree at the left with a full crown, built in three passes (dark masses, leaves, lit leaves) so it never looks bare; leaves on the sidewalk and a few falling (`.fl`, off with reduced motion).
   - **Colour**: daylight colours are pulled towards dusk with `D(hex)`; anything lit keeps its own colour, then glows are added with screen blending.
   - Coordinates keep the 1536 by 1024 frame and the door is at x 696 to 860, y 484 to 860, hinged on the right, so the swing, the zoom and the doorway line up. The drawing sits at the bottom of the screen (`xMidYMax meet`); on tall phones `fitFacade` narrows the view to the shop's width. Behind the door is a live copy of the interior (`fillDoorway`).
3. **Shop interior**: one 1920 by 1080 SVG in true one-point perspective. Everything is placed in the room in world units (2.4 to the centimetre at the back wall; x across, y down with the floor at 900, z depth with 0 at the back wall and negative towards the viewer) and projected through one vanishing point at eye height (960, 470) with `PJ(x, y, z)`. `box()` draws only the faces you could really see, `recess()` draws cubbies with their insides, `plane(z)` scales anything flat facing the viewer, and `disc()` lays circles flat. Use these for anything new so the perspective stays consistent.
   - **Layout** (from the owner's reference images): the pressing booth stands in the middle of the room, and everything else is the record wall around it. The owner photobashed a wall of lit album cubbies under paper lanterns as the rest of the room, and picked the Birdman Records listening bar as the booth. Earlier layouts (record wall on the left and the booth in the right-hand corner, then an open corner booth) were replaced.
   - **Expansive**: the owner found the first version boxed in next to their reference. The record wall now runs off the top and both sides of the picture on every screen shape: the ceiling (`CEIL`) and side walls (`WALL_L`, `WALL_R`) sit out of view, so only the wall, the oak floor and the booth show. Keep it that way: no visible ceiling or room corners.
   - **Look**: Japanese wood interiors, moody and warm, never like an office: dark walnut joinery, an oak board floor, all colour coming from the album covers, and the light coming from inside the cubbies and from paper lanterns. No daylight, no orange. A light amber multiply layer warms the room without muddying it, with soft gradient glows (`lampG`), a warm wash over the wall and a soft vignette. Rejected along the way: plain pale or brown walls (sterile), orange, photo-booth or kissing-booth shapes, enclosed booths.
   - **Record wall**: the whole back wall, cut into lit walnut cubbies at real size (about 34 cm), 30 across and 12 high, running past the edges of the picture, each lit by a warm strip under its top edge with light falling down its back. About six in seven hold a real album face out (names on hover); the rest hold a stack, a record, spines or a small plant. Cubbies hidden behind the booth's shelving aren't drawn.
   - **Side counters** along the wall, left and right of the booth: cabinets of record spines, a hi-fi speaker, an amp and clocks below; a reel-to-reel, two turntables and a mushroom lamp (red on the left, yellow on the right) on top. Keep the record players few: one on each side counter and one on the booth. Their cubbies are shallow (back at `SC.zf + 34`) with the records, amp, clock and speaker standing at their backs, so both counters look the same; deep cubbies showed big pale floors and mismatched rows and were rejected.
   - **Pressing Booth** (centre, after Birdman Records): walnut shelving standing in front of the wall, tall (from y 250, the proportions the owner preferred; a lower version was rejected), with a big speaker in a dark bay at each end, lit shelves of leaning records, and in the middle a lit sign box ("Side B", "PRESSING BOOTH") over two silver power amps and a receiver. In front is a long counter with a slatted walnut front, a thick steel top lit from beneath and a glass screen along its back edge. On it: one silver turntable under its lid, the small record press (blue base, steel posts) in the middle, headphones and a red lamp. A long cylinder paper lantern hangs above it. No stools and no note (both removed at the owner's request). Clicking any of it opens the builder. Hovering it brightens it with a soft warm glow (CSS filter on `#booth`), never an outline box. The lantern's and the sign's glows are `nohit`, so they don't block hover names on the covers behind them.
   - **Hanging**: twelve bright round paper lanterns, big and small, kept high in the upper part of the wall and clear of the booth (lanterns hanging low were rejected) (`LANTERNS`, placed by where they appear on screen and turned into room positions), plus a monstera in each front corner.
   - The room is drawn for 1920 by 1080, and `fitShop` widens or deepens the view to fill other screen shapes. Phones scroll across it, starting centred on the booth in the middle. Tapping the booth zooms into the middle of the room.
4. **Builder (the "studio")**: modelled on the Vinylacy configurator (vinylacy.com/pages/configurator-beta). The owner screen-recorded themselves making a record there and wants its flow, layout, options, variety and motion, but not its look. Rejected along the way: menus of parts inside steps, a layout that changes between steps, 11 to 13 tiny steps, and a stripped-down 5-step version that took away too many options.
   - **One frame for every step**: the record on a dark stage (left; on top on phones), a cream card of choices beside it (below it on phones), and back, "Step 3 of 6", a thin progress line and a big round next button (Press on the last step) floating at the bottom of the stage.
   - **The look** (the shop at night, minimal, so the record stands out): no solid bars. The stage is dark walnut (`--stage`) under a warm pool of lamplight, with no work mat or props. The logo, the step names and "Back to the shop" float over it in cream; back and next float over the stage (on phones, in a slim dark pill over the bottom of the card). The choices sit on a cream card with rounded corners and a soft shadow. Accents are cream (`--cream`) and lamp amber (`--amber`). The solid paper header and footer bars were rejected as blocking. All of this is the "THE BOOTH'S LOOK" block at the end of the styles. The header names every step; click one to jump to it. Tapping the step's name in the bar lists every step with its current setting.
   - **Motion** (the record moves to show what's being worked on): out of the sleeve, centred and gently floating for Shape and Vinyl (`data-mode="disc"`), turning with a quick tilted spin whenever its look changes (`spinRecord`); zoomed right into its label on a dark stage for Labels (`data-mode="label"`, the label drawn big on its own with `labelURL` in `#pLabel`, flipping when you switch sides); back in the sleeve for Sleeve, turning over for the back. Panel content slides in with each step, and the chosen option's name and a line about it show on the bench (`#caption`; in the panel on phones). The record also answers the pointer: it tilts towards the mouse; on Shape and Vinyl you can grab it and spin it, and it keeps turning then slows; with the sleeve showing, dragging sideways swings it and a long drag turns it over (`hold`, `tiltTo`, `coast`). This is off while words or drawing are being edited. Shaped records' tiles have no background behind them. Reduced-motion settings turn the animations off.
   - **Six steps** (`FLOW` in the code), in this order (the owner wants the look made first and the songs chosen after):
     1. **Shape**: round, heart, star, hexagon, octagon, with a line about each (`OPT_NOTES`). The size follows the songs added later.
     2. **Vinyl**: three kinds (`VCATS`): Classic (black, solid colour, half and half, colour in colour, swirl, transparent), Premium (metallic, iridescent, smoke, marbled, ultra clear, eco-mix) and Picture disc on its own (choosing that kind selects it straight away). A picture disc carries its own uploaded picture (`draft.pdisc`, 300 px so links stay short), or the cover picture if none is uploaded, or a "Your picture goes here" placeholder. Then main and second colour dots (14 named colours plus "Any colour"). New records start as classic black. Splatter was removed; older splatter records show as marbled.
     3. **Labels**: Side A and Side B tabs. Two ready-made labels to start from (`LABEL_LAYOUTS`: Classic, the record's name and side on plain paper; Two-tone, the same on two colours), a paper colour, then the layer editor. Double-clicking a ready-made label (or "Change the printed words") turns its printed words into editable words in the same places (`labelToLayers`). Add text and Add image are the main ways to make a label your own (a picture is kept at 240 px so links stay short, with a size stepper and "Fill the label"). Stored per side in `draft.lab.A` / `draft.lab.B` as `{ texts, img, z, st }`. There is no separate style list any more.
     4. **Sleeve**: Front and Back tabs. Front: two choices (`COVERS`): **Upload a picture** (with 10 filters) or **Draw on it** (a blank sleeve; on desktop the brushes, colours, eraser, undo and clear sit in a bar on the bench by the sleeve, `#drawBar`, not in the panel). **On phones drawing opens a full-screen pad** (`#drawSheet`, `openSheet`/`closeSheet` move the bench's own canvas and tools into it and back): the sleeve as wide as the screen, big Undo, Clear and Done along the top, all colours in two rows and the brush sizes and eraser below, nothing scrolling off the edge, the page held still. The bench then shows one "Keep drawing" button. The cramped strip of tools under a small sleeve was rejected. Clear can be undone: Undo brings a cleared drawing back. Then two finishing touches (obi strip, hype sticker with its three lines) and the layer editor for words. No colour sets, no plain-colour sleeves, no worn edges, stereo banner or 180 gram badge. Back: credits, half-speed mastered logo, barcode (no legal small print).
     5. **Songs**: search songs (emptying the search box clears the results), add to side A or B, reorder with arrows, a "to B"/"to A" button on every song, and drag and drop within and between the sides. Minimum 6 songs to press. 6 to 10 songs makes a 10-inch, 11 or more makes a 12-inch LP, up to 12 per side. The back of the sleeve shrinks the tracklist to fit however many songs there are.
     6. **Ready to press**: the record's name and who it's from and for (gift tag, link preview, back of the sleeve, printed label), a recap of every step (tap a line to go back to it) and the tracklist. Pressing needs 6 songs, and a picture if the cover is "Upload a picture".
   - **Picture tiles** sit in balanced rows (`renderDock` sets the columns: at least 3, so 6 make two rows of 3, 5 make 3 and 2, 10 make 4, 4 and 2), and every group in the panel has the same spacing above and below it.
   - **Panel spacing** follows one rhythm: 22px above each group's heading and 10px between a heading and its choices. Colour dots sit in even rows of at most 7 (`dotCols`), two-option cards sit two across, and the text editor and label picture controls each sit in their own light card. Nothing touches the group above it.
   - **The panel** is wide (`clamp(410px, 38.5vw, 530px)`) so nothing is cramped. Avoid horizontal sliders in it: sizes and turns use − / + steppers that repeat when held.
   - New records start with nothing written on the sleeve.
   - **The layer editor** (`surf()` says which surface is open: the sleeve front or a label side): Add text and Add image, the selected words' text (or double-click words on the preview to type into them), size and turn steppers, six fonts as "Ag" tiles, colour dots, Bring forward, Send backward, Duplicate, Delete, and a Layers list (top first, the picture at the bottom). While a surface is open it's drawn without its words, and the words sit over it as real text (`renderTextLayer`) with handles to move, resize (showing its size) and turn, and × to remove.
   - **Start over** and **Turn it over** sit at the top of the bench. Start over keeps the old record reachable with "Carry on with your last record".
   - **Fixed spots, so nothing overlaps**: the stereo banner runs across the top, the obi strip down the left, the sticker sits top right and the badge bottom right. The back is a fixed layout: header, tracklist, credits, and a bottom row with the small print, the half-speed logo and a real EAN-13 barcode (20 to 29 in-store prefix).
   - **Older records** still draw everything they were made with (the old title, line and credit, notes). Colours are stored as hex; older records that stored list positions are converted when opened; older records without a cover type get "your picture" if they have one and "plain colour" if not.
5. **Receiving it**: a kraft record bag with a name tag opens onto a listening room seen straight down onto a wooden table, so everything shares one view: a stack of real records with a vinyl peeking out, a plant from above, window light and sun shadows, a silver turntable. The sleeve and a card sit on the left, the turntable in the middle, a receiver on the right. Pull the sleeve forward, flip it to read the tracklist and notes, slide the record out, carry it to the turntable.

## How the record is drawn

The owner wants every record to look like the real thing, whatever its shape or finish: realistic material, texture and light, not a flat illustration. `drawVinyl` builds it in layers:

- **Material** (`vinylBase`): each finish is worked out point by point with noise (`makeNoise`, `fbm`): marbling and swirl that flow round the record, a soft pool for colour in colour, a wavering seam for half and half, metal flake, pearl, smoke hanging in clear plastic, a muddy recycled mix for eco. Transparent, ultra clear and smoke are really see-through (the canvas keeps its alpha). Older finishes (splatter, clouds, stripe) are still drawn for old records.
- **Grooves**: hundreds of fine rings, each a touch lighter or darker, with a smooth gap between songs (as many gaps as the side has songs), the dead wax and run-out groove round the label, and the lead-in and bead at the edge.
- **Light**: the lamp is up and to the left. In the grooves the light gathers into two bright wedges (`lobes`), broken into streaks by the grooves, with a faint rainbow either side on dark, metallic and pearl records. Smooth parts (dead wax, the border of a shaped record) get one soft sheen. See-through records glow at the rim. The edge is bevelled, bright towards the lamp and dark away from it. No drawn outline anywhere.
- **Label**: paper grain, a pressing ring, a little shadow at its rim, and a spindle hole.

Rejected: flat fills with a few drawn rings, a black outline round the record and the label, and random straight lines for marbling. To check changes, draw a sheet of every finish and shape side by side at 720 px.

## The turntable (everything works)

Power knob, start/stop, 33 and 45 speeds (45 plays faster and higher), pitch slider (plus or minus 8 percent), target light, cue lever, dust cover, tonearm drag (needle position chooses the song, each ring is a track), hold the record to slow it down, tap the label to flip sides. The receiver has volume, tone and balance knobs and live VU meters.

Playback: each song is a 30-second preview. Real songs play the Apple preview through an `<audio>` element (playback rate follows the platter speed, pitch is not preserved). Practice songs are synthesised in the browser with the Web Audio API.

## Data and sharing

- The record (songs, design, words, photo, and notes on older records) is JSON, compressed with `CompressionStream('deflate-raw')` and base64url encoded. That packed record is what gets stored or put in a link.
- Short links: when Upstash is connected (Vercel sets `KV_REST_API_URL` and `KV_REST_API_TOKEN`), pressing stores the packed record plus a small sleeve picture under `rec:<id>` with a 30-day expiry and shares `/r/<id>`. Each record is about 14 KB (more with a photo), so the free tier (256 MB) holds about 18,000 at a time. After 30 days the link shows "This record has come off the shelf".
- If storage isn't set up or doesn't answer, pressing falls back to packing the record into the link itself (below), so sharing never breaks.
- New share links put the record after `?r=`, because some apps and link redirects drop the part after `#`. A record with a photo is too long for that and goes after `#r=`. Old `#r=` links still open. On load, `?r=` is moved into `#r=` and handled the same way.
- Links are unpacked with `DecompressionStream` where the browser has it, and with a small built-in inflater (`inflateRaw`) where it doesn't (iPhones before iOS 16.4, some in-app browsers).
- Links pressed on a Vercel branch preview point at that preview, which other people may not be able to open. The share dialog says so.
- The draft being built is saved in the browser's `localStorage` under `sideb-press`. Every visit starts a new record. If the last one had songs or a name, the booth shows "Carry on with your last record". After pressing, "Make another record" starts fresh. Someone who receives a record gets "Make one back", which starts a new record addressed to the sender.
- Sound: one button mutes everything, and the choice is remembered (`sideb-muted`). Outside there's a quiet low street hum. Inside the shop it's silent: the owner disliked white noise and then each generated ambient piece (an electric piano, then a dream-pop piece after Mazzy Star). `ambStart`/`ambStop` still exist but nothing calls `ambStart`. The builder and the listening room are silent apart from the records themselves.
- When the site isn't on a real web host (for example a local file or the Claude preview), search falls back to a practice catalogue of made-up albums and songs generated in code.

## Style

The look is a **rich, sunlit illustration**, taken from the Ultima Thule shop illustration the owner supplied: flat shapes with no outlines, strong afternoon sunlight from the upper left, hard-edged cool shadows (`rgba(24,30,70,.3)`) that fall down and to the right, light shafts through windows, a fine grain over everything, lots of small real-world detail (plants, lamps, crates, posters, tiles), and real album covers wherever covers appear. Objects show their top and side faces so they read as solid, not flat.

Scale matters: draw things at their real sizes relative to each other (see the shop interior notes above).

Palette: navy `#1F3A63` (lighter `#2E5488`, darker `#152A4A`), sign yellow `#EDB33A`, red `#B3252B`, orange walls `#D86A2C`, terracotta `#C4572B`, brick `#B9533A`, cream stone `#E8DCC1`, woods `#C98A4E` `#9A5A2C` `#6B3A1A`, plant greens `#2F6238` `#3E7A45`, burgundy `#7B2A2E` for interface buttons.

Type: Archivo (grotesk), Archivo Narrow (the small labels and details in the interface; `--mono` now points at it), Caveat (handwriting), Yellowtail (sign-painter script), DM Serif Display. No monospaced fonts in the interface (the owner's request). IBM Plex Mono (`--typewriter`) stays only as part of the record itself: the "Mono" choice for words, the printed label and the back of the sleeve.

UI language for the builder: a dark lamplit stage with floating cream controls, and a cream paper card for the choices (see "The look" above).

Avoid: black ink outlines, soft glossy gradients standing in for lighting, objects at the wrong size for each other, made-up album covers where real ones fit, childish cartoon styling.

## Writing rules for anything on the site

- No em dashes.
- Plain, warm, human language. No jargon, no filler phrases.

## Known limits and ideas for later

- Records expire after 30 days. The free Upstash database is archived if nothing touches it for 14 days (restorable from the Upstash console).
- The shelves use the covers saved in `img/covers/`. Practice covers only appear if that saved list is empty. 55 of the 71 albums in the list are saved. The other 16 aren't in Apple's album search in India or the US (Blonde, channel ORANGE, The Dark Side of the Moon, Nevermind, IGOR, Flower Boy, Swimming, Circles, Faces, Awaken My Love!, Freudian, Is This It, Baduizm, Isolation, Punisher, For Emma Forever Ago), so they simply don't appear; swap them for albums Apple has if they're wanted.
- A person browsing the record wall was in the reference images but left out until it can be drawn well.
- Performance: the turntable redraws only when something changes. Keep it that way.
